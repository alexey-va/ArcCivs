# Resident life, 0.1.31

The owner requested five automatic arrivals, families above five, hunger and instant worker summoning. This changes workforce access and food consumption, not currency payouts.

Before: four one-time pioneers; replacement recruitment costs 12 bread; hall population caps 4/8/12 and house beds 2/4/6. After: recurring immigration fills vacancies below five, caps 5/8/12 and beds 3/4/6. A pair in a completed house needs 20 active city minutes per child, children need 40 active minutes to become workers. No offline catch-up. Total city cap remains twelve, minors count against it. Long-term production per completed action and paid native inputs remain unchanged. Level-one maximum workforce increases by 25%, while higher-level caps stay unchanged. Free replacements make deliberate deaths cheaper, but destroy the resident's actual equipment and require arrival time; they do not return items or money.

One active working adult loses one food per minute and consumes existing bread at food <=14, restoring six. Steady upper upkeep is ten bread per working hour; the initial food reserve delays this cost. Food <=6 doubles work duration. Manual feeding still consumes one bread and can restore up to six health and six food; it cannot mint bread. Children do not work or drain working food. Consumption is saved with the snapshot before publishing; a rejected save cannot consume food twice.

The JSON and generated result separate bread, resident rights, Vault and tokens. Low/base/high are assumptions: 1/10/16 cities, one working adult for 1/2/4 active hours daily. Multiply upkeep by each city's actual simultaneously working adults (at most twelve); do not add alternative occupations as simultaneous full-time earnings. Initial arrivals are shown only for the founding day, not as daily recurring issuance. Hunger can halve throughput; precise income effects depend on travel and actual jobs and are not claimed as measured. Summon saves travel but preserves the working duration and input receipts. One supplied item comes from the player's inventory. Household growth provides no saleable items.

Read-only ledger on 2026-10-20 covered 168 hours, 31 players and 31,083 operations. Currency-tagged records were used; mixed legacy totals were excluded. Vault mint is dominated by two admin grants (67,001,499 coins), so it is not a normal player-income baseline. Tokens include five unknown-origin debit operations; the ledger does not measure city bread or native production. Current SELL remains a possible redemption route, while the planned contract-only economy is a separate future scenario. No conversion rate for bread or residents is invented; there is no direct Vault or token flow in this change.

Accepted because the requested early workforce is bounded at five and total capacity remains twelve, birth/maturation require active time, all normal supplies use existing items, and food adds an explicit ongoing sink. Validate population caps, restart-safe family progress, one-time food consumption, hungry timing and no child assignments in focused tests. Delivery/readiness and native-client acceptance are separate evidence; Survival must not be restarted.

The owner additionally requested a faster farmer: one native farming action changes from 3s to 1.5s (hungry: 3s). The theoretical fed action ceiling doubles from 1200 to 2400/hour; soil, native growth, routes and commit latency constrain real output. This does not create additional drops per block or accelerate crop growth. The JSON tracks actions in a separate unit, without pricing them as harvested items or money. Combined level-one workforce and action ceilings can reach 2.5x the old theoretical all-farmer action rate, but actual harvest income has not been measured. Keep this explicit limit in acceptance; do not infer income from the admin-dominated ledger.
